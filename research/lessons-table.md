## Lessons for Toronto (each tied to sources)

1. **A charge reduces traffic, and capacity has to absorb it.**
   - Stockholm's bus expansion explained ≤0.1 points of the 22% drop (B-STO-005), but the expanded transit absorbed shifted trips (B-STO-004).
   - London's buses carried +38% at launch (B-LON-004).
   - Singapore's 1975 park-and-ride failed (B-ALS-009).
   - **Toronto:** pair the charge with frequent regular transit (bus priority, service) on the corridors feeding the zone, not fringe parking.

2. **Price the shoulders and the bypasses.**
   - ALS shoulder +23% and hours extension (B-ALS-004).
   - NYC 05:00/21:00 spikes (B-NYC-007).
   - Stockholm's free Essingeleden was eventually priced (B-STO-013).
   - London's uncharged Inner Ring Road was managed operationally (B-LON-002).
   - **Toronto:** model time-varying shoulder rates (scenario S3) and decide the Gardiner/DVP question explicitly.

3. **Volume and delay are different outcomes.**
   - London's congestion relief fell from 30% to 8% while volumes stayed down, because road space was reallocated (B-LON-008).
   - **Toronto:** report both, and set a freed-road-space policy before launch.

4. **Operating cost falls with automation and maturity, so budget a worse net in years one and two.**
   - London 76% → 35% (B-LON-012).
   - Stockholm about SEK 220M estimated → €22M actual (B-STO-007/011).
   - NYC about 20% in year one with modern tolling (B-NYC-003).
   - **Toronto:** use these as a reasonableness range only. Build collections bottom-up.

5. **A stronger congestion effect means less revenue.**
   - London expected a revenue shortfall because traffic fell faster than forecast [U].
   - Stockholm's elasticity *increased* over time (B-STO-012).
   - **Toronto:** the financial model must show the trade-off.

6. **Earmarking decides the politics.**
   - London fences revenue by law (B-LON-010).
   - NYC bonds it to a $15B capital plan (B-NYC-017).
   - Stockholm's 50/50 package deal was "a turning point" (B-STO-014).
   - Gothenburg's increase, imposed to co-finance a project with BCR 0.45, eroded support (CTS 2017:2 p.20).
   - **Toronto:** earmark visibly, and only for projects that serve the people who pay.

7. **Support rises after people experience the benefits, so sequence a trial.**
   - Stockholm: 55% negative → 53% positive → 53% referendum yes (B-STO-008/009).
   - NYC: 32/56 → 42/35 in two months [U] (B-NYC-014).
   - **Toronto:** a time-limited trial with open evaluation. The City cannot do this alone: since June 2025 it needs an Act of the Ontario Legislature (City of Toronto Act s.41 as re-enacted; s.116 repealed).

8. **Surrounding municipalities oppose a charge that the core city supports.**
   - Stockholm suburbs voted 40% yes (B-STO-009).
   - NYC's pause was linked to suburban seats (B-NYC-016) [U].
   - **Toronto:** this is the "commuters from the west/905" concern raised in user feedback (UF-001). Map the burden by approach direction.

9. **Exemptions are gamed and expensive.**
   - ALS taxi exemption reversed after three weeks; car-pool exemption abused (B-ALS-001, Wikipedia [U]).
   - About 30% of Stockholm trial passages were exempt (B-STO-002).
   - **Toronto:** cost each exemption explicitly.

10. **Publish a rate rule.**
    - Singapore's speed-band, quarterly, small-step rule keeps changes technocratic (B-ERP-003).
    - NYC's legislated increases ($12 in 2028, $15 in 2031) are set in advance but exposed to politics (B-NYC-011, CBC slide 34).

11. **Implementation risk is mostly institutional, not technical.**
    - Stockholm's cost was inflated by redundancy built against political risk (B-STO-011).
    - Procurement litigation shortened the trial (Eliasson WCTR).
    - London had contractor service problems (B-LON-016).
    - Singapore ran a zero-charge shakedown period (B-ERP-004).

12. **Penalty income is large and should not be assumed.** London's penalties were 27% of FY2007/08 revenue (B-LON-010).

## Where Toronto's own evidence should override the foreign analogies

- **Baseline traffic and crossings:** Toronto midblock and intersection counts and the boundary geometry, not foreign entry volumes.
- **Mode alternatives by approach direction:** Toronto TTS public summaries, Census and TTC/GO service. Do not use Stockholm's 75% transit share or NYC's subway density. NYC's and Stockholm's high transit shares made their equity case easier (Komanoff, TRB slide 38; CTS 2017:2 p.12).
- **Diversion geometry:** Toronto's open grid, and the choke points that do exist (the lake, the Don Valley, rail corridors), simulated in SUMO. NYC's rivers and Stockholm's water barriers do not transfer.
- **Institutional path:** Toronto's own 2015 toll study (EX8.14) and City of Toronto Act s.41 (re-enacted 2025 as an outright bar), not London's mayoral powers.
- **Revenue:** chargeable events × rates from Toronto data. Foreign net/gross ratios (NYC about 0.80 in 2025; London 0.51 in FY2007/08) are reasonableness bounds only.

## Corrections to `data-sources.md` found in this workstream

- **NYC 2025 finances.** The primary report gives toll revenue **$700.5M**, total revenue $701.2M, expenses **$139.2M** and net **$562.0M** (preliminary; mta.info/document/197586 p.2). The figures $716.5M / $138.1M / ~$578M are not supported.
- **NYC speeds.** "+11%" is the NBER causal estimate for *cordon-area roads* (Google Maps), not a measured zone-wide speed. Other measures range from +1% to +15%.
- **London year one.** Four-or-more-wheel vehicles entering fell **18%** and **cars 33%**. `data-sources.md` has these the wrong way round (25%/18%); 25% is the drop in potentially-chargeable vehicle-km inside the zone.
- **Singapore ALS.** The 44% (all vehicles) and 73% (cars) reductions are confirmed for 07:30–10:15 only. "Zone speeds from under 20 to 33 km/h" is unsupported: before-period speed data were unusable, and 33 km/h was compared with 27 km/h in the evening. "74,000 → 41,500" was not found. The zone was about 500 ha at launch, not 670 ha.
- **Singapore ERP source.** "Chin (2010), SIM Series 33" is in fact Menon & Guttikunda (2010). Capex S$197M, O&M S$25M/yr, revenue S$144M (2008–09).
- **Stockholm.** Trial hours were 06:30–18:30 and about 30% of trial passages were exempt. The ITF revenue/opex series (31% → 7%) is UNVERIFIED (source blocked).
