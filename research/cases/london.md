# London: Central London Congestion Charge, launched 17 February 2003

Workstream B case file. Researched 2026-10-24. Source IDs refer to `research/sources_B.csv`.

## Charge design and changes over time

**Launch design**
- **Launch:** 17 February 2003, £5 per day flat.
- **Hours:** 07:00–18:30 on weekdays.
- **Zone:** about 21 km² (UNVERIFIED), bounded by the Inner Ring Road, which is itself uncharged and serves as the bypass.

**Changes since**
- **2005:** £8 (B-LON-017 context).
- **19 February 2007:** the Western Extension is added, and hours are shortened to 07:00–18:00 the same day (Sixth Report §10.16, p.219 [PDF p.226]).
- **4 January 2011:** the Western Extension is removed after Mayor Johnson's statutory consultation (65% of about 13,000 respondents wanted it removed; B-LON-014).
- **2 January 2026:** the current charge of **£18** takes effect (£21 if paid within 3 days). Hours are 07:00–18:00 weekdays and 12:00–18:00 weekends and bank holidays (B-LON-015).
- **25 December 2025:** the blanket electric-vehicle exemption ended. It was replaced by 25% off for electric cars on Auto Pay and 50% off for electric vans and HGVs (B-LON-015, UNVERIFIED: press).
- **Residents:** have historically received a 90% discount.

## Traffic, speed and reliability outcomes

**Year 1, 2003 vs 2002.** Annualised weekday traffic during charging hours (Second Report Table 3.1, p.27 [PDF p.31]):

| Vehicle type | Inbound change | Outbound change |
|---|---|---|
| All vehicles | −14% | −18% |
| Four or more wheels | **−18%** | −21% |
| Potentially chargeable (cars, vans, lorries) | −27% | −29% |
| Cars | **−33%** | −35% |
| Vans | −11% | −15% |
| Licensed taxis | +17% | +8% |
| Buses and coaches | +23% | +21% |
| Two-wheelers | +15% | +5% |

- Vehicle-km inside the zone: −12% for all vehicles, −15% for four or more wheels, and **−34% for cars** (Table 3.3, p.29 [PDF p.33]).
- **Congestion**, measured as excess travel time, fell **30%** (Overview p.1–2). "Time spent stationary or moving slowly in queues" fell by up to one-third.
- **Inner Ring Road:** small traffic increases but no operational problems. TfL found **no systematic increase in traffic on local roads outside the zone** (Overview p.3).

**Correction to `data-sources.md`.** It says "vehicles with 4+ wheels entering fell about 25% in year one, cars about 18%". The primary report shows the reverse: **four or more wheels −18%, cars −33%** (inbound). The 25% figure is the vehicle-km reduction for *potentially chargeable* vehicles inside the zone.

**Behavioural decomposition (Overview p.4).** Of 65,000–70,000 car trips no longer made to the zone:
- 50–60% shifted to public transport;
- 20–30% diverted around the zone (through-trips);
- 15–25% made other changes (timing, car share, etc.).

Only about 5,000 individuals stopped visiting the zone at all (§6.5, p.90). Label: ESTIMATED.

**Five years on (Sixth Report)**
- Traffic entering, 2007 vs 2002: −16% all vehicles, −21% four or more wheels, −29% potentially chargeable (§3, p.39 [PDF p.47]; Table 3.1).
- **Congestion relief eroded:** −30% in 2003–04, −21% in 2005, and **only −8% in 2006** vs 2002 (Overview p.3 [PDF p.10]).
- TfL attributes the erosion to:
  - road space reallocated to other priorities (bus lanes, pedestrian schemes, signal timing changes);
  - an accelerated utility works programme;
  - development and construction;
  - a long-standing background trend.
- TfL argued that a static 2002 baseline had become "increasingly inappropriate".

**Western Extension removal, 2011.** In the first 12 weeks of 2011 vs 2010, traffic entering the former zone in charging hours rose **8%**, and speeds were up to 3% slower (TfL press release, June 2011; B-LON-014).

## Transit outcomes and complementary investment

**Bus service was expanded at launch.** In a typical autumn 2003 weekday AM peak, **106,000 passengers entered the zone on 560 buses** (Overview p.3).
- That is **+38% patronage and +23% bus service** vs 2002.
- About half of the patronage increase is attributed to the charge.
- In the zone, excess waiting time from irregular service fell **30%**, delay-related disruption fell **60%**, and bus speeds rose 6%.

**Underground.** AM-peak station exits in the zone fell 8% (513,000 → 473,000). TfL attributes this mostly to non-charge factors, including the 2003 Central line closure (Overview p.3).

**National Rail** was broadly static.

## Capital and operating costs

**Setup cost.** About £161.7M is widely cited (B-LON-013, **UNVERIFIED**: secondary blog quoting a 2007 report). Pre-launch projections were £180M NPV start-up and £320M NPV operating (CESifo DICE 2005, UNVERIFIED).

**First-year operating cost: £141.4M against £186.7M revenue (76%)** (TfL 2004, cited in Börjesson & Kristoffersson CTS WP 2017:2 p.10; B-LON-012).

**Early cost-benefit estimate.** Table 6.1, Second Report p.91 [PDF p.95], "preliminary", from *Six Months On*:
- Annual costs: £130M, of which scheme operation £90M, additional buses £20M, TfL administration £5M and chargepayer compliance £15M.
- Annual benefits: £180M.
- Net: about £50M per year. Label: MODELLED.
- Charge payments are correctly excluded as transfers.

**Later benefit–cost ratio.** The Fifth Report estimate is about 1.5 at £5 and about 1.7 at £8 (Sixth Report p.7 [PDF p.14]; B-LON-017).

**FY2007/08, provisional** (Sixth Report Table 10.2, p.220 [PDF p.227]; B-LON-010):

| Item | £M |
|---|---|
| Operations, publicity, enforcement | 91 |
| TfL staff, traffic management, central costs | 40 |
| **Total costs** | **131** |
| Standard daily charges (£8) | 146 |
| Fleet charges (£7) | 37 |
| Resident charges (£4/week) | 12 |
| **Enforcement income (penalties)** | **73** |
| **Total revenues** | **268** |
| **Net revenues** | **137** |

Penalties were **27% of total revenue**. Toronto's model must decide whether enforcement income counts as revenue, and must not assume it.

**FY2015/16.** Operating cost £90.1M on £258.4M revenue (35%) (TfL 2016 via CTS 2017:2 p.10).

## Use of revenue

By law, net revenues must be spent on measures that further the Mayor's Transport Strategy (Scheme Order appendix; Sixth Report p.220). In FY2007/08 the £137M net was allocated:
- bus network £112M;
- roads and bridges £13M;
- road safety £4M;
- walking and cycling £4M;
- borough plans £2M;
- environment £2M.

## Technology, procurement and implementation

- **Technology:** fixed ANPR cameras; drivers paid by retail outlet, phone, SMS, web or post.
- **Contractor:** Capita was the primary service provider.
- **Early service problems.** In the first months, Capita's service quality was "below the standard required". A Supplemental Agreement brought a programme of IT, management, process and staffing improvements and a tougher performance regime (Second Report Overview p.5).
- **Volumes.** About 550,000 payments per week in 2003. By 2007, compliance was 96% and satisfaction 82% (Sixth Report p.8; B-LON-011).

## Exemptions and mitigation

- Residents get a 90% discount (consistent with £4/week vs £8/day in FY2007/08, Table 10.2).
- Blue Badge (disability) holders are exempt (UNVERIFIED this session).
- Buses, licensed taxis and emergency vehicles are exempt.
- Alternative-fuel/cleaner-vehicle discounts applied until 2025.
- NHS staff, patients and care workers can claim reimbursement (TfL page; B-LON-015).

## Public acceptance, communications, political and legal

- **Introduced by Mayor Ken Livingstone** under the Greater London Authority Act 1999, which gave the Mayor charging powers. There was no referendum.
- **Business view.** Most businesses in or near the zone were "generally supportive" by 2004. Retailers' claims of large losses were judged inconsistent with the scale of reduced travel (Second Report Overview p.4).
- **Main political reversal.** Johnson's 2008 manifesto led to the 2011 Western Extension removal. "West Londoners never wanted the extension… had it foisted upon them" (B-LON-014, press). Lesson: an extension imposed without local buy-in can be undone.
- **Elsewhere in the UK (UNVERIFIED).** Edinburgh (2005) and Manchester (2008) referendums rejected congestion charges.

## What worked

- **A large, immediate and persistent drop in cars entering**: −33% in 2003, still −21% for four or more wheels in 2007.
- **Bus expansion on launch day plus freed road space** gave a visible year-one win: +38% AM-peak bus passengers and −30% excess waiting time.
- **Displacement was smaller than feared.** No systematic increase on local roads, and Inner Ring Road increases were manageable.
- **Revenue is fenced for transport by law.**

## What didn't work

- **Delay came back.** Congestion relief fell from 30% to 8% by 2006 because road space was reallocated and dug up. TfL argues the counterfactual would have been worse, but the public sees congestion.
- **Very high early operating cost** (76% of revenue in year one), driven by manual payment channels and contractor performance problems.
- **Revenue was below forecast.** Traffic fell faster than expected, and TfL anticipated a £65M revenue shortfall within six months (CESifo, UNVERIFIED). A strong behavioural response means less money.
- **The Western Extension was reversed** after a statutory consultation.

## What remains uncertain

- The counterfactual for post-2006 congestion.
- Long-run attribution, given trends after 2010 (ride-hail growth, freight).
- Setup cost (not verified from a primary source).

## Relevance for Toronto

- **Track volumes and delay as separate outcomes,** and decide in advance who gets the freed road space.
- **Automated payment from day one** (plates plus accounts). Avoid manual channels.
- **Budget for a low net in years one and two.**
- **Penalty revenue** is a policy choice, not a windfall.
- **Have bus capacity in place on launch day.**
