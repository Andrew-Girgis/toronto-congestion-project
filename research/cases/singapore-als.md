# Singapore: Area Licensing Scheme (ALS), launched 2 June 1975

Workstream B case file. Researched 2026-10-24. Source IDs refer to `research/sources_B.csv`.

**This is a separate intervention from ERP (1998)**: a different era, technology and policy package. ALS is treated as historical evidence, **not a prediction for Toronto**.

**Primary source read this session:** Holland, E.P. & Watson, P.L., "Measuring the impacts of Singapore's area license scheme", World Bank Urban Projects Department, *WCTR 1977* (Rotterdam), pp. 417–421. The source PDF is https://wctrs-society.com/wp-content/uploads/abstracts/rotterdam/Segment%20050%20of%20WCTR77_Rotterdam%20(3).pdf. Cited here as "H&W". It is the World Bank's before/after evaluation, also published as Holland & Watson (1978).

## Charge design and changes over time

**Original design (H&W pp. 417–418; B-ALS-008)**
- A paper licence displayed on the windscreen.
- **S$3/day or S$60/month**, later raised to S$4/day and S$80/month.
- Required to enter a **Restricted Zone of about 500 ha with 22 entry points** in the morning: 07:30–09:30, extended to **10:15** soon after launch because congestion appeared after 09:30.
- Policy goal: **cut peak-hour traffic by 25–30%** (H&W p.418; B-ALS-012).
- **Exempt:** buses, commercial vehicles, motorcycles and **car pools (4+ occupants)**. Taxis were initially exempt; the exemption was rescinded after three weeks.

**The package** was more than a licence (H&W p.418):
- about **+100% parking charges** at public car parks in the zone (B-ALS-010);
- a **park-and-ride scheme** with 10,000 fringe spaces and shuttle buses (B-ALS-009);
- a ban on large three-axle vehicles in peak;
- a campaign for staggered hours and car pooling.

**Later changes (secondary sources, UNVERIFIED)**
- Licence raised to S$5 (1980), then back to S$3 (1989).
- Evening peak added (1989).
- Car-pool exemption abolished (1989).
- Full-day 07:30–18:30 (1994).
- Zone enlarged (sources give about 6 km², later 7.25 km²; `data-sources.md` says 670–720 ha). This conflicts with H&W's "about 500 hectares" at launch. Use H&W for 1975.
- **Replaced by ERP in September 1998.**

## Behavioural response

From the household panel: 2,053 households interviewed before and 4–6 months after (H&W p.420; B-ALS-007).

**Commuters from vehicle-owning households working in the RZ**
- Car share of trips fell **56% → 46%**.
- **Bus share rose 33% → 46%**.
- The car-pool share of car trips **tripled, from 14% to 41%**.
- Work trips started before 07:30 rose from 27% to 40% (car drivers).
- Roughly equal numbers chose bus, car pool and retiming.

**Through-zone (trans-RZ) commuters**
- 88% drove through the zone before. Afterwards 66% did, and only 13% of those during restricted hours. The rest retimed.
- Many bypassed the zone via the ring road in the morning but drove home through the zone in the evening, which explains the small PM drop.

**Shopping trips** to the RZ fell 34%, but trips outside the zone also fell 14%. The ALS-attributable share is "unlikely… more than about 5 per cent" for parking charges. The rest is recession and decentralisation.

**Non-vehicle-owning households** did not change behaviour: 90% travelled by bus before and after.

**Secondary, UNVERIFIED (B-ALS-015):** transit share of work trips into the RZ rose from 33% before ALS to about 70% by 1983 (Yap 2005 via Wikipedia). If used, it must be labelled as bundled with fleet restraint and transit growth.

## Transit and complementary measures

- **Park-and-ride "attracted very few patrons"** (H&W p.418). The fringe car park and shuttle (S$30/month) largely failed.
- **Regular buses absorbed the shift.** Bus share among affected commuters rose 13 points.

## Costs, revenue and economics

- **Investment** covered fringe car parks, shuttle facilities, signs and ticket booths (buses excluded). No total was found this session (UNVERIFIED).
- **First-year rate of return ≥ 15%**, counting only net travel-time savings valued at S$1/hour, about 25–30% of the average wage (H&W p.421; B-ALS-011). Label: MODELLED.
- **Revenue was not the objective.** General-revenue era; no ring-fencing.

## Technology and enforcement

- Paper licences sold at post offices and kiosks, checked visually by wardens at entry points.
- **No electronic metering.** That limitation prevented per-trip pricing (see ERP).

## Public acceptance, communications, political

- Designed and implemented by an inter-ministerial Road Transport Action Committee (H&W p.417), with more than a year of advance notice.
- **No referendum or electoral test.** Singapore's governance context differs fundamentally from Toronto's.
- **Equity framing.** The car-pool and motorcycle exemptions were explicitly justified as countering "objections that driving into the center becomes a luxury only the rich can afford" (H&W p.418).

## What worked

- **A very large, immediate reduction** in cars entering the zone in the charged window (−73%).
- **Car pooling and bus absorbed the demand.**
- **Simple and cheap to administer.**

## What didn't work

- **Shoulder bunching.** Cars entering just before 07:30 rose 23%, and congestion appeared after 09:30, forcing the hours extension to 10:15 within weeks.
- **Bypass congestion.** The ring road was about 20% slower in the AM.
- **Evening peak barely changed** (−6%), because a morning-only charge doesn't price the trip home.
- **Park-and-ride failed.**
- **The taxi exemption backfired** and was rescinded after 3 weeks. The car-pool exemption was later abused (pick-up of bus commuters) and abolished (UNVERIFIED, secondary).
- **Likely over-priced.** Phang & Toh (2004) note the reduction exceeded the 25–30% target (secondary summary, UNVERIFIED).

## Remaining uncertainty

- **Before-period speeds** (unusable data).
- **Long-run attribution**: COE ownership quotas from 1990, MRT from 1987 and other factors confound everything after the early years.

## Relevance for Toronto

1. **Price the shoulders and the bypasses from day one.** This is the strongest transferable lesson; NYC 2025 shows the same pattern at 05:00 and 21:00.
2. **A morning-only or one-direction charge leaks**, because people drive in early and drive home free.
3. **Exemptions get gamed.** Design them narrowly with an enforcement plan.
4. **Fringe park-and-ride is not a substitute** for frequent regular transit.

**Do not use ALS magnitudes (−44%/−73%) as Toronto predictions.** The fleet, era, transit market share and instrument are all different.
